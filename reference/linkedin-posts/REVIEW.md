## Second note: serials / cereals, decided before building (2026-11-28)

What the post has: the joke (a native hears *cereals*, хлопья), three
Russian words and their English ones (сериал → TV series / TV show, серия →
episode, серийный → serial: serial killer, serial number), and one grammar
oddity (one series, two series). The slide is the joke as a picture.

- **Hook: the joke, in the hero, as type.** The figure is the ladder the
  grammar notes use (no new component): what you mean (Russian), what you
  say (*serials*, specimen ink), what they hear (*cereals*), what to say
  (*TV shows*, target ink). The caption carries the sound: serial and
  cereal are one sound, /ˈsɪəriəl/. The carousel image itself stays off the
  site (NOTES-PLAN §3: no carousel images).
- **Headline:** "Series, / not cereal." / «Сериалы, / а не хлопья.» It
  echoes actual's "Real, / not current." and keeps the sound pun
  (series / cereal).
- **Standfirst:** the post's opening, then the answer a searcher came for:
  "сериал is a TV series or a TV show; серия is an episode."
- **01 The fix: three Russian words, three English ones.** A cheat-sheet
  table: сериал, серия, сезон (DRAFT: not in the post, but the next word
  people need), серийный. The lede: "Only серийный is serial."
- **02 Why it happens:** what the hero doesn't say. Why a native hears
  breakfast (one sound, two spellings), and why серия isn't series
  (Russian серия is one part, English series is the whole run), with the
  post's own oddity as pull rows: один сериал → one series, два сериала →
  two series, одна серия → one episode.
- **03 Practice: the same test as actual** ("Which would a native speaker
  say?"), eight sentences, five wrong, three right; a miss names the
  Russian phrase behind it; the score goes to Telegram with the note's
  name.
- **Search:** «сериал по-английски», «серия по-английски», «series
  множественное число», "serial or series". Title and description carry
  them.
- **Drafts** for Igor (NQ14): the English translation, the сезон row, the
  why lede, the test sentences not taken from the post.

## Third note: key + abbreviations as one, "API key", decided before building (2026-11-28)

Two posts, one phrase. «Апи кей» carries both mistakes in two words: the
abbreviation read as a word (апи, сео; the abbreviations post, 25 Nov) and
key said as the name of the letter K (the key post, 30 Oct). For IT people,
who are a large share of Igor's readers, it is a phrase said daily.

- **Kind: Pronunciation.** Practice is "Say it out loud", as on the other
  pronunciation notes (au, stress); the test is for vocabulary.
- **Hook: the slide's A·P·I as tiles** (the au note's figure, no new
  component). Row one, written: A, P, I, and key as one wide tile. Row two,
  said: эй, пи, ай, ки, in target ink. The picture is the rule: three
  letters, one word. Caption: say эй-пи-ай ки, not ~~апи кей~~.
- **Headline:** "Three letters / and a key." / «Три буквы / и один ключ.»
- **Standfirst:** the posts' observation, then the answer: API is spelled
  out, эй-пи-ай; key is ки.
- **01 The fix:** the posts' eight words in one table, written / say /
  transcription: CEO, API, MR, PR, key, hotkey, keyboard, keynote. A
  footnote (DRAFT) keeps the rule honest: a few short ones are read as
  words (RAM, PIN), so "2-3 letters: spell it" is a rule of thumb.
- **02 Why:** the posts give the effect (апи, сео; кей = the letter K);
  the note adds the cause (DRAFT): Russian speakers name Latin letters the
  school way, а, бэ, цэ, и, ка, so the English names are new: A is эй,
  E is и, I is ай, R is ар, and кей is K. Pull rows: the school name →
  the letter → its English name.
- **03 Practice:** six sentences from a working day (DRAFT), the
  abbreviations and key words in bold; stress falls on the last letter.
- **Drafts** for Igor (NQ15): the translation, the footnote, the why, the
  sentences.

## Fourth note: weigh / weight, decided before building (2026-11-28)

The post is in English (the only one of the eight), so this time the
Russian is the translation, a DRAFT. Its content: weight is a noun people
know, nouns often work as verbs in English, so people say "I weighted
80 kg", "It weights 2 pounds"; drop the t, weigh /weɪ/, the same sound as
way. The slide: VERB, NOT NOUN; WEIGH with the T falling off; "Doing it?
Drop the t."

- **Kind: Grammar** (noun vs verb), practised with the test: judging
  sentences is what the mistake needs.
- **Headline: the slide's own line.** "Doing it? / Drop the t." /
  «Это действие? / Уберите t.»
- **Hero: the au tiles again, the slide's picture as type.** W E I G H T
  with the T in specimen ink, over W E I G H in target ink, its sixth cell
  empty: the t is gone. Caption: I ~~weight~~ 80 kg → I weigh 80 kg.
- **Standfirst:** the post's opening ("I've seen this mistake a thousand
  times"), ending on the answer: weight is the thing, weigh the action,
  said like way.
- **01 The fix:** a table of what Russian says and what English says
  (вешу, весил, весит, сколько весите, взвесить, мой вес), and one honest
  row (DRAFT): a weighted average, where weighted is a real word with
  another meaning. That is the likely reason it sounds right to people at
  work.
- **02 Why:** the post's reason, nouns working as verbs (you email
  someone, you text them), as pull rows ending on the exception: весить →
  to weigh, not to weight.
- **03 Practice:** the ear test, eight sentences, five wrong; one right
  one is "We used a weighted average", so the test teaches the twist.
- **Drafts** for Igor (NQ16): the Russian translation, the table rows not
  in the post, the weighted-average row, the why's pull rows, the test
  sentences.

## Fifth note: manage to, decided before building (2026-11-28)

The post (1 October; text and slides in `BACKLOG.md`): most B1 learners
know manage only as управлять; its more useful meaning is manage to,
справиться, получилось, "when it wasn't easy but you did it": I'll manage,
I managed to fix the bug. Slide 2 is Igor's own exercise, three sentences
to put through manage to (задеплоить до пятницы, я справлюсь, не
получилось договориться с клиентом); its answers were never posted.

- **Kind: Vocabulary**, practised with the test, like actual and serial.
- **The specimen (DRAFT).** The post names the gap, not the wrong form.
  What fills the gap is *could*: «смог» becomes "I could fix it", which in
  English means "I was able to, in general", not "I did it, once". So the
  ledger row is I ~~could~~ deploy by Friday → I managed to deploy by
  Friday, slide 2's first sentence.
- **Headline:** "Not just / for managers." / «Не только / для менеджеров.»
  The post's own turn: the word people know is the manager's word.
- **Hero: actual's dictionary entry**, no new component. manage
  /ˈmænɪdʒ/; you know: управлять; it also means: справиться, получилось;
  say: I managed to. Caption: the post's definition, it wasn't easy, but
  you did it.
- **Standfirst:** the post's opening, ending on the answer: manage to is
  справиться, получилось.
- **01 The fix:** Russian → English, the post's two (я справлюсь, у меня
  получилось пофиксить баг), then rows people need (DRAFT): еле успел,
  как вы справляетесь, сможете в пятницу?
- **02 Why (DRAFT):** school teaches could as мочь, so смог becomes could too; but could is мог.
  Could is ability in general (в пять лет я мог читать), managed to is one
  success that took effort (я смог починить). Not managing is easy: both
  couldn't and didn't manage to are right. Pull rows, one per job.
- **03 Practice:** the ear test. Slide 2's three sentences go in as
  English, one wrong (could) and two right, plus four more (DRAFT). One
  right sentence is "We couldn't agree on a price", so the test teaches
  the twist, as weighted average did on weigh.
- **Search:** «удалось по-английски», «справиться по-английски», «manage
  to перевод», "could or managed to". Title and description carry them.
- **Drafts** for Igor (NQ18): the English translation, the could
  specimen, the table rows not in the post, the why, the test sentences
  beyond slide 2.
