*Visual identity only. Voice, positioning, and content strategy live in the
main coaching-content project — paste this into other projects when the task
is producing media (logo, carousels, banners, slides, decks) that needs to
look like it belongs to the same brand.*

---

## What Vitnyr is

Vitnyr is the umbrella brand unifying Leon's three coaching disciplines —
English for IT professionals, chess, and climbing — under one identity built
on a deliberate-practice / learning-science methodology. It exists to
position Leon as a multi-dimensional performance coach, not "just an English
teacher," and to give his public presence (LinkedIn Company Page, etc.) a
proper identity instead of a generic freelancer placeholder.

---

## Color system

Two fixed pairs. Choose by contrast against the background — never by mood,
never mixed, never a third color introduced.

**Pair D — on dark** (backgrounds darker than ~40% relative luminance, e.g.
charcoal `#14181A`)
- Amber `#D99B3F`
- Green `#4C7A52`

**Pair L — on light** (backgrounds lighter than ~65% relative luminance,
e.g. cream `#EFEBE3`)
- Amber `#B07C24`
- Green `#3D6543`

Pair L is not a lightened Pair D — it's *darker*. The values are Pair D taken
down until they hold 4.5:1 contrast against cream. This is counter-intuitive
the first time; it's still the rule.

**On anything mid-tone** (photographs, mid-grey, anything that isn't clearly
dark or clearly light): use the one-colour mark — white or charcoal,
whichever holds 4.5:1. Never place a colour pair on a mid-tone background.

**Approved backgrounds: charcoal and cream only. No plain white.**

---

## Typography

Lora (serif). Used for the wordmark and any headline-level brand moment.
Wordmark is set in tracked-out caps: **VITNYR**.

---

## The wordmark

`VITNYR` in tracked-out Lora caps. The **V is amber**, the **Y is green**,
every other letter is neutral (cream on dark backgrounds, charcoal on light).
This is deliberate — the wordmark visually teaches the mark by highlighting
the same two letters in the same two colors the mark uses.

---

## The mark

A single fused glyph: a V drawn in amber, with a green vertical stem
descending from the vertex to complete a Y. One continuous shape, not two
letters side by side. It should never need the "V and Y fused" logic
explained to a viewer — it just needs to work as one clean shape at a glance.

**One-colour fallback:** identical geometry, single fill (white or
charcoal). Loses the amber/green distinction but keeps the silhouette.
Use for embroidery, stamps, single-colour print, or anywhere the mark needs
to survive very small or in one ink.

**Favicon / small-size cut:** below ~24–28px, don't just scale down the
standard mark — it turns muddy (amber and green antialias into one
indistinct color). Use a simplified cut instead: shortened arms, thickened
stroke, built specifically for tiny sizes.

---

## Clear space & minimum size

- Clear space: at least ¼ of the mark's height, on all four sides. Nothing —
  type, rules, edges, other icons — enters that margin.
- Two-colour minimum: 32px on screen / 9mm in print.
- One-colour minimum: 16px on screen / 6mm in print or stitched.
- Below the two-colour minimum, switch to the favicon cut rather than
  shrinking the standard mark further.

---

## Hard rules — what this brand never does

- No gradients, no drop shadows — flat colour only
- No plain white backgrounds
- No stock-photo or generic-SaaS visual aesthetic
- No third colour, under any circumstance
- No recoloring the mark to match a host platform, template, or partner brand
- No stretching, skewing, or rotating the mark or wordmark
