# Notes council: review and work order

**Date:** 2026-11-20. **Reviewed:** `main` at `de3bfd6` (SITE-PLAN stages
1–5 merged) with `feature/notes` at `85a56d5` merged on top, in a scratch
copy. Nothing in `main` was changed by this review.

**What this is:** Igor asked for a council (developer, designer, copywriter,
marketer) to go over the whole site, log what could be better in the notes
feature and in the bigger picture, cross-check it, throw out the weak ideas
and keep the best, including big ones. This file is that log, already cut

it again.

**Read with:** `NOTES-PLAN.md` (what was built and why), `SITE-PLAN.md`
(the site's work order; its "Not doing" and "Decided later" lists were

- All seven pages (home, English, chess, climbing, `/notes/`, the two
  notes), in English and Russian, light and dark, at 375px (touch) and

- The full test suite on `main` + notes: **432 passed, 4 failed, 1 known

## 1. The verdict

The notes are the right feature and the right look: they are the first
thing on the site that speaks to the exact people already reading Igor on
LinkedIn, and the typeset figures (tiles, weights, ledger) belong to the same
engraved, specimen-and-target world as the rest of the site. Three things
hold them back. **(1)** They were built on the morning's `main` and the
afternoon's `main` moved under them: their masthead, footer, contact block,
fonts and scripts are now a version behind. **(2)** As pages, they end in a
big ask (a trial lesson) when a pronunciation note has a natural small one
(say these six and send them to me). **(3)** Two notes is a thin library
and nothing on the site points to it except one link on the English page.

The bigger picture adds one real bug, found on the way: the chess page's
"Is your rating actually stuck?" tool returns nothing for most players.

**K2. Put the generator in the repo, and make it copy the shell.** · M ·
developer
The three pages were generated by a script that lives in a temporary

**K3. Copy and accuracy fixes.** · S · copywriter, with a linguist's eye ·
partly Igor
- "the **au cluster**" is the wrong word: a cluster is a run of consonants.
  Say "the **au spelling**" (EN). The Russian «сочетание au» is right as is.
- **Accent footnote** on the au note: the page says au is always /ɔː/.
  That is British. Many Americans say /ɑː/ (Merriam-Webster gives both for
  *launch*). The point of the note survives in both accents (one vowel,
  never а+у), so add one line: "British /ɔː/; many Americans say /ɑː/.
  Either way: one vowel, not two." Without it, the first US-based reader
  who checks a dictionary stops trusting the page.
- The stress note opens "the same way they do with **CONFLICT**": that
  points at an earlier LinkedIn post the site doesn't have. Either make
  CONflict/conFLICT its own short note later, or say "the same way they do
  with CONflict and conFLICT" so the sentence stands on its own.
- Contact copy on both notes says "trial lesson" twice in a row ("The trial
  lesson is a good place to find out. The 30-minute trial lesson is
  free…"). Second sentence becomes "It's 30 minutes, free, and you owe me
  nothing after it."
- Carried from NOTES-PLAN §8, still open: **conSOLE** means "to comfort",
  not an IT action (Q7: swap for REcord/reCORD?); **"95%"** is Igor's own
  figure, not one of the confirmed facts in CLAUDE.md (Q2: keep, or "almost
  all"?).

### Tier 2: the growth engine

**K5. "Send me your six."** · S · marketer, copywriter · Igor (yes to the
wording)
The English page already has an approved smaller step: "Send me a
60-second voice message". On a pronunciation note the step writes itself.
End each "Say it out loud" block with a Telegram link prefilled in the
reader's language:
- au: "Record these six sentences as a voice message and send them to me.
  I'll tell you which one slips."
- stress: the same, for the twelve phrases.
It costs nothing to the reader, gives Igor a real sample of their English,
and starts the conversation that a trial lesson needs. The prefill names the
note, so Igor knows where the lead came from (SITE-PLAN A2).

**K6. Igor's voice on every word.** · M · designer, marketer · Igor
(recordings)
The single biggest gap in a pronunciation page is that it can't be heard.
Robot voices were rejected (NOTES-PLAN §3) and should stay rejected; Igor's
own voice is the opposite case: it is the product. Six words and six
sentences per note, recorded on a phone, about 15–25 KB each as `.m4a`. A
small play glyph at the end of each register row and practice line, in the
existing ink, no autoplay, no waveform, nothing moving. Plays one clip at a
time; works with the keyboard; the clip's text is the button's label.
Without JS the rows read exactly as today.

Igor (approves drafts)

### Tier 3: the bigger picture

**K10. Fix the chess rating check (found during this review).** · S ·
developer · its own branch, now
The chess page's "Is your rating actually stuck?" calls Lichess's
`/api/user/<name>/rating-history`. On 2026-11-20 that endpoint returned `[]`
for well-known public accounts (EricRosen, penguingim1), so the page tells
almost every visitor "Lichess isn't sharing a rating history for that
username right now". SITE-PLAN's status table already suspected this. The
`/api/user/<name>/perf/<speed>` endpoint works, is open to other sites
(CORS `*`) and returns the current rating, the **peak and when**, the
number of games and the recent progress. For "is it stuck?", *current vs
peak* answers the question even better than a line chart. For EricRosen
on 2026-11-20 it gave: blitz 2585 now, peak 2714 (April 2025), 9,449 games.
Keep the chart when the history comes back; fall back to current vs peak
when it doesn't.

**K11. One source, two outputs: the carousel comes from the note.** · M ·
designer, marketer
Igor's carousels already share the site's language (ТАБЛИЦА IX, Фиг. 1,
РЕЕСТР). Reverse the workflow: write the note once in K2's data file, then
export the LinkedIn carousel (1080×1350 slides, PDF) from the same figures,
the way `assets/share/share-card.html` already exports the share cards. The
person who swiped the carousel lands on a page that looks exactly like it,
and Igor stops designing every post twice.

**K12. Catalogue numbers.** · S · designer
Number the entries like a collection: "No. 01" on each ledger row and in
each note's eyebrow, newest highest. It uses Igor's own register language
and gives a reason to follow the series. The title stays "Notes from the
lessons": warmer than "Register" for someone arriving from LinkedIn.

**K13. The empty right half of the notes index hero.** · S · designer

right. Put the newest note's figure there (today: the L-A-U-N-C-H tiles),
linked to that note. The index then shows what a note looks like before the
reader scrolls.

**K14. One spelling throughout.** · S · copywriter
The four main pages mix British and American: *memorised* ×1 but
*memorized* ×3; *Over-gripping* ×3, *Overgripping* ×1, *over-grip* ×1. The
site's figures are British (*analysed*, IELTS), so: *memorised*,
*over-gripping*. The meaning of Igor's copy doesn't change.

**K15. IPA that a screen reader can say.** · S · developer
`/lɔːntʃ/` is read out symbol by symbol. Give each IPA cell an `aria-label`
in plain words ("sounds like lawnch") and keep the IPA visible.
